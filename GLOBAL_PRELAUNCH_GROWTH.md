# NETVERSE Global Pre-Launch Growth Plan

Goal: build a real international community before public launch without fabricating traction, customers, revenue or technical readiness.

## Positioning

NETVERSE — Connect. Compute. Create.

NETVERSE is building a Web + Android platform that combines:
- reviewed AI agents and paid AI services;
- creator commerce through BuilderZone;
- verified payments and access;
- consent-based distributed compute;
- authenticated community, support and notifications.

Primary public line:

AI agents, creator commerce and verified distributed compute — one platform.

## Five audiences

1. Early users who want AI agents, Premium tools and useful digital products.
2. Builders who may publish agents, tools and products.
3. Compute contributors willing to test Android bounded workloads.
4. Partners, newsletters, media and technical communities.
5. Investors and advisors who understand AI infrastructure, marketplaces and distributed systems.

## Growth narrative

### AI agents as real products
NETVERSE is building reviewed and versioned AI-agent products with verified paid access, lifecycle controls and operator-managed runtime configuration.

### Creators need distribution
BuilderZone is designed around explicit ownership and marketplace settlement so creators can publish useful digital products and AI agents inside the same ecosystem.

### Distributed compute should be accountable
NETVERSE does not treat online status as paid work. Contributor attribution is intended only for completed and independently validated workloads.

### One economic layer
Payments, access, service consumption, marketplace settlement and verified compute accounting are designed to connect instead of living in isolated products.

### Built from Bulgaria for a global market
NETVERSE is being developed from Bulgaria with international users, builders, contributors, partners and media in mind from the beginning.

## Channel strategy

### X / Threads / Bluesky
Cadence: 1–3 useful posts per day.
Use milestones, screenshots, technical threads, founder notes, builder questions and beta recruitment.
Avoid fake urgency, repeated identical links and unsupported scale claims.

### LinkedIn
Cadence: 3–5 posts per week.
Use product thesis, milestone breakdowns, founder journey, partner/advisor asks and investor-friendly progress summaries.

### Reddit
Post selectively and follow subreddit rules.
Best formats: technical questions, architecture discussions, build logs and requests for critique.
Do not mass-post identical promotional copy.

### Hacker News
Use only for meaningful technical milestones.
Good formats: Show HN, architecture write-ups, postmortems and technically interesting design decisions.

### Product Hunt
Prepare maker story, demo video, screenshots, first-comment narrative, clear user problem and a launch-day supporter list.

### Indie Hackers
Use founder logs, progress metrics, product decisions, experiments, failures and fixes.

### DEV / Hashnode / Medium
Publish technical explainers about verified compute, payment verification, AI-agent versioning, marketplace integrity and Android architecture.

### YouTube / TikTok / Reels / Shorts
Create 20–60 second videos around one idea:
- What is NETVERSE?
- Why uptime alone earns nothing.
- AI agents as paid products.
- What BuilderZone is.
- Why Android compute is opt-in.
- What we verified this week.

### Discord / Telegram
Use for build updates, polls, tester recruitment, community Q&A and ambassador coordination.

### GitHub
Use as the public source of truth: roadmap, status, launch checklist, waitlists, milestones and transparent limitations.

## 30-day campaign

### Week 1 — Make NETVERSE understandable
- pin a one-line description everywhere;
- publish What NETVERSE is / is not;
- publish one architecture overview;
- recruit ambassadors and translators;
- ask builders what they would publish.

### Week 2 — Show proof of work
- publish verified milestone summaries;
- share real product screenshots;
- publish a technical article on verified distributed compute;
- recruit paid-service testers;
- recruit Android compute testers.

### Week 3 — Build honest social proof
- share real community feedback with permission;
- publish builder interviews;
- run an AMA;
- contact startup newsletters and technical creators;
- start partner conversations.

### Week 4 — Prepare launch momentum
- publish launch narrative;
- release short demo clips;
- build Product Hunt supporter list;
- prepare community-specific HN and Reddit posts;
- publish ready/not-ready launch status;
- schedule launch-day and launch-week content.

## Weekly content engine

Every verified engineering milestone should create:
1. one X post;
2. one LinkedIn post;
3. one screenshot or visual;
4. one Discord update;
5. one status/changelog update;
6. one optional technical article when educational.

Marketing should come from real engineering progress, not invented stories.

## Metrics

Track:
- waitlist signups;
- qualified tester applications;
- active Discord members;
- GitHub stars/watchers;
- builder applications;
- compute beta applications;
- partner/media conversations;
- post saves/replies;
- referral source;
- weekly active community contributors.

## Integrity rules

Never claim:
- revenue that is not settled and verified;
- waitlist members as customers;
- compute income from heartbeat/uptime;
- millions of devices without evidence;
- partnerships that are not agreed;
- investor interest without permission;
- public launch while launch gates remain open.

Credible progress is the long-term growth advantage.
